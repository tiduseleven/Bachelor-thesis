\section*{Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Introduction}

Homological algebra is a key aspect of many modern topics in mathematics, be it geometry, algebra or topology. While originally only studied on modules and abelian groups, it was then later formalized on abelian categories, giving a more abstract framework. Exact categories, first introduced by Quillen, are a continuation of this abstraction, reducing the requirements to the bare minimum while still enabling discussing short exact sequences. In an attempt to also study longer exactness and to axiomatize properties of so-called $n$-cluster tilting subcategories of exact categories, a generalization to $n$-exact categories was introduced by Jasso. This thesis aims to build the necessary tools to study such structures, while also providing a few examples.

First, we will generalize the structure of short exact sequences to additive categories, motivated by fundamental properties arising in abelian categories. We will then proceed with some examples of exact categories by finding extension-closed subcategories. Finally, we will give a smaller list of axioms that is equivalent to the classical definition.

Then, we will extend the concepts studied to longer exact sequences. We will define the necessary higher-degree concepts in order to define $n$-exact categories, and proceed with a few elementary results. To conclude, we will give some examples of $n$-exact categories by looking at $n$-cluster tilting subcategories.

This thesis does not have a direct connection to sustainability. However, it is worth noting that quality education is one of the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals. In particular, abstract algebra—and mathematics as a whole—fosters critical and rational thinking, which are essential skills for addressing complex sustainability challenges.

In order to understand this thesis, the reader should have some knowledge about modern homological algebra. In particular, they should already be familiar with category theory, more specifically abelian category theory, as is presented for example in the lecture notes of MA3204 Homological Algebra \cite{homalg}.

For the last part of the thesis, the reader should also be familiar with Auslander-Reiten theory and AR-quivers, as is for example explained in \cite{Assem}.
