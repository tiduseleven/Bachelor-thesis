\section{An introduction to exact categories} \label{chapter:exact}
As a way to generalize the notion of abelian categories, one can look at a general additive category on which we define a certain notion of short exact sequences that has very similar properties to the abelian category counterpart. This chapter aims to motivate the definition of these so-called exact categories, to later define them formally and give a few concrete examples. We will also give a minimal version of the axioms due to B. Keller.

The content of this chapter is inspired by the lecture notes of MA3204 Homological Algebra \cite{homalg}, the article about exact categories by T. Bühler \cite{Buhler2010Jan} and B. Keller's notes on exact categories \cite[Appendix~A]{Keller1990}.

\subsection{Motivation} \label{section:chapone_motivation}
When studying abelian categories, there are many convenient properties related to monomorphisms and epimorphisms, which are used to prove results about short exact sequences. Let us recall a few:

\begin{prop} \label{prop:popb_abelian}
    In an abelian category, 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item every monomorphism is a kernel of its cokernel;
        \item every epimorphism is a cokernel of its kernel.
    \end{itemize}

    Furthermore, the following properties hold:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item the pushout of a monomorphism is a monomorphism;
        \item the pullback of a monomorphism is a monomorphism;
        \item the pushout of an epimorphism is an epimorphism;
        \item the pullback of an epimorphism is an epimorphism.
    \end{itemize}
\end{prop}

We refer the reader to \cite[Sections~12-13]{homalg} for the proofs of these facts.

Fundamentally, this tells us that every monomorphism and every epimorphism fits into a short exact sequence, and that taking the pullback or pushout of a morphism that is part of a short exact sequence gives rise to another short exact sequence. Indeed, if $f: A \rightarrowtail B$ is a monomorphism, then it fits into the short exact sequence $\ses{A}{B}{\Cok{f}}{f}{\cok{f}}$. Furthermore, since morphisms that are part of a short exact sequence are either monomorphisms or epimorphisms, they will once again be a monomorphism or an epimorphism after a pullback or pushout, thus will once again fit in a short exact sequence.

Let us quickly recall what it means for a class of composable morphisms to be closed under isomorphisms.

\begin{defn}
    Let $\cat{E}$ be a class of composable morphisms, i.e. a set of pairs $(f,g)$ where the codomain of $f$ is the domain of $g$. We say $\cat{E}$ is \emph{closed under isomorphisms} if, for any commutative diagram

        A \& B \& C \\
        {A'} \& {B'} \& {C'}
        \arrow["f", from=1-1, to=1-2]

    where $(f,g) \in \cat{E}$ and the vertical maps are isomorphisms, $(f',g') \in \cat{E}$.
\end{defn}

There are many properties we can conclude about kernels and cokernels using the previous results and this definition. These properties are what will motivate the definition of exact categories.

\begin{prop} \label{prop:ab_is_exact}
    Let $\cat{A}$ be an abelian category and let $\cat{E}$ be the class of short exact sequences. $\cat{E}$ is closed under isomorphisms. Furthermore, the following hold:
    \begin{itemize}[align=right]
        \item[\textnormal{[E0]}] For any object $A \in \cat{A}$, the identity morphism $1_A$ is a kernel;
        \item[\textnormal{[E0${\op}$]}] For any object $A \in \cat{A}$, the identity morphism $1_A$ is a cokernel;
        \item[\emph{[E1]}] The class of kernels is closed under composition;
        \item[\emph{[E1${\op}$]}] The class of cokernels is closed under composition;  
        \item[\emph{[E2]}] The pushout of a kernel along any morphism is a kernel;
        \item[\emph{[E2${\op}$]}] The pullback of a cokernel along any morphism is a cokernel.
    \end{itemize}
\end{prop}

\begin{preuve}
    It is clear that the class of short exact sequences is closed under isomorphisms. Indeed, $\ses{A}{B}{C}{f}{g}$ is a short exact sequence if and only if $f$ is a kernel of $g$ and $g$ is a cokernel of $f$. The result follows from the fact that kernels and cokernels are preserved by isomorphisms.

    [E0] and [E0${\op}$] follow directly from the fact that the identity morphism is both the kernel and cokernel of the zero map.

    We can deduce [E1] and [E1${\op}$] by recalling that composing monomorphisms (respectively epimorphisms) gives us monomorphisms and that monomorphisms are kernels of their cokernel (respectively epimorphisms are cokernels of their kernel).

    As for [E2], recall that kernels are always monomorphisms. Thus, by \cref{prop:popb_abelian}, the pushout of a kernel is a monomorphism as well. But then, by \cref{prop:popb_abelian} again, it is a kernel of its cokernel, thus it is a kernel.
    The proof for [E2${\op}$] is similar.
\end{preuve}

\begin{rmk}
    We can in a very similar way prove that the pullback of a kernel (respectively the pushout of a cokernel) is a kernel (respectively cokernel), however we choose not to include this as it turns out to be unnecessary for many results.
\end{rmk}

\subsection{Exact categories}
Based on the observations from the last section, one can define a more general notion that encapsulates the idea of short exact sequences, namely exact categories. To do so, let us first define kernel-cokernel pairs.

\begin{defn}
    Let $\cat{A}$ be an additive category. A \emph{kernel-cokernel pair} $(i,p)$ in $\cat{A}$ is a pair of composable morphisms $A \xra{i} B \xra{p} C$ such that $i$ is a kernel of $p$ and $p$ is a cokernel of $i$.
\end{defn}

In the context of abelian categories, we notice that kernel-cokernel pairs indeed give rise to short exact sequences in the usual sense. In a general additive category, however, we cannot say the same since we have no guarantee that kernels and cokernels even exist for arbitrary morphisms, and thus we are unsure that $\Img{i}$ exists. But we will use our intuition from abelian categories to expand the notion of short exact sequences to additive categories. First, let us fix some terminology.

\begin{defn}
    Let $\cat{A}$ be an additive category and $\cat{E}$ be a class of kernel-cokernel pairs in $\cat{A}$. We call a morphism $i: A \xra{} B$ an \emph{inflation} (or \emph{admissible monomorphism}) if there exists a morphism $p: B \xra{} C$ such that $(i,p) \in \cat{E}$. Dually, we call a morphism $q: B \xra{} C$ a \emph{deflation} (or \emph{admissible epimorphism}) if there exists a morphism $j: A \xra{} B$ such that $(j,q) \in \cat{E}$. We use $A \xrat{i} B$ for inflations, and $B \xrath{p} C$ for deflations. We say $\cat{E}$ is \emph{closed under isomorphisms} if whenever we have $A \xra{i} B \xra{p} C \in \cat{E}$ fitting in a commutative diagram

We can now proceed to define exact categories, basing our definition on the properties we established for abelian categories.

\begin{defn}
    Let $\cat{A}$ be an additive category. An \emph{exact structure} on $\cat{A}$ is a class $\cat{E}$ of kernel-cokernel pairs in $\cat{A}$ that is closed under isomorphisms and satisfies the following axioms:
    \begin{itemize}[align=right]
        \item[{[E0]}] For all objects $A \in \cat{A}$, the identity morphism $1_A$ is an inflation;
        \item[{[E0${\op}$]}] For all objects $A \in \cat{A}$, the identity morphism $1_A$ is a deflation;
        \item[{[E1]}] The composition of two inflations yields an inflation;
        \item[{[E1${\op}$]}] The composition of two deflations yields a deflation;
        \item[{[E2]}] The pushout of an inflation along any morphism yields an inflation;
        \item[{[E2${\op}$]}] The pullback of a deflation along any morphism yields a deflation.
    \end{itemize}

\subsection{Extension-closed subcategories}
In \cref{section:chapone_motivation}, we saw that all abelian categories are exact categories, with a possible exact structure being all short exact sequences. We can generalize this example of exact categories to extension-closed subcategories of abelian categories. Let us first clarify what this means.

\begin{defn}
Let $\cat{A}$ be an abelian category and $\cat{C}\subseteq \cat{A}$ be a full additive subcategory of $\cat{A}$. $\cat{C}$ is said to be \emph{closed under extension} if, for any short exact sequence in $\cat{A}$

\[\begin{tikzcd}[ampersand replacement=\&]
	0 \& A \& B \& C \& 0
	\arrow[from=1-1, to=1-2]
	\arrow[from=1-2, to=1-3]
	\arrow[from=1-3, to=1-4]
	\arrow[from=1-4, to=1-5]
\end{tikzcd}\]

such that $A$ and $C$ are both in $\cat{C}$, then $B$ is also in $\cat{C}$.

In that context, we say that $B$ is an \emph{extension of $C$ by $A$}.
\end{defn}

\begin{prop}
    Let $\cat{A}$ be an abelian category and $\cat{C} \subseteq \cat{A}$ be a full additive subcategory of $\cat{A}$ that is closed under extensions. Let $\cat{E}$ be the collection of short exact sequences in $\cat{A}$ such that all three objects are in $\cat{C}$. Then $\left(\cat{C}, \cat{E}\right)$ is an exact category.
\end{prop}

\begin{preuve}
    We prove each of the axioms one by one.

    $\cat{E}$ is closed under isomorphisms since short exact sequences in $\cat{A}$ are closed under isomorphisms and $\cat{C}$ is full. 

    Since $\cat{C}$ is an additive subcategory, it contains the zero object. As such, for any object $B \in \cat{C}$, the sequences $\ses{0}{B}{B}{}{1_B}$ and $\ses{B}{B}{0}{1_B}{}$ are short exact and contained in $\cat{C}$, i.e. identity morphisms are both deflations and inflations.

    Now let us consider the axiom [E2]. Let $X \xrat{i} Y \xrath{d} Z$ be a short exact sequence in $\cat{C}$ and $f: A \xra{} X$. We can consider the pushout diagram in the ambient category $\cat{A}$:

    where $C = \Cok{j}$. From basic results in abelian categories, we know that $j$ is a monomorphism, and that we can choose $C = Z$ (see \cite[Proposition~13.7]{homalg} for the latter). Thus the sequence $A \xrat{j} B \xrath{e} Z$ is short exact, with both $A$ and $Z$ in $\cat{C}$, which implies that $B \in \cat{C}$ since it is extension-closed. We therefore get a short exact sequence in $\cat{C}$, and $j$ is an inflation. The proof for [E2$\op$] is dual.

    To prove [E1], let us consider short exact sequences $A \xrat{i} B \xrath{d} C$ and $B \xrat{j} Y \xrath{e} Z$ in $\cat{C}$. Then we can construct the following pushout in the ambient category:

    \end{tikzcd}\]
    By \cref{prop:popb_abelian}, we know that $f$ is an epimorphism. It then remains to prove that $j \circ i$ is a kernel of $f$. Since $j$ is a monomorphism, one can show that the square is also a pullback (see \cite[Corollary~13.6]{homalg} for a proof). 
    Consider $g: X \xra{} Y$ such that $f \circ g = 0$. Using the pullback property on $g: X \xra{} Y$ and $0: X \xra{} C$, we get a unique map $\hat{g}: X \xra{} B$ such that $j \circ \hat{g} = g$ and $d \circ \hat{g} = 0$.
    Now using that $i$ is a kernel of $d$, we get a unique map $\hat{\hat{g}}: X \xra{} A$ such that $i \circ \hat{\hat{g}} = \hat{g}$. In particular, $(j \circ i) \circ \hat{\hat{g}} = g$, and $\hat{\hat{g}}$ is the unique map satisfying this property. Finally, we note that 
    \[f \circ (j \circ i) = (f \circ j) \circ i = (k \circ d) \circ i = k \circ (d \circ i) = 0\]
    so that we can conclude that $j \circ i$ is a kernel of $f$. Since $f$ is an epimorphism and $j \circ i$ is its kernel, we conclude that $A \xrat{j\circ i} Y \xrath{f} E$ is a short exact sequence in $\cat{C}$. As such, inflations are closed under composition. Proving [E1$\op$] is dual.
\end{preuve}
\begin{rmk}
    A slightly modified converse statement is also true, and is known as the \emph{Gabriel-Quillen embedding theorem}. It states that, if $(\cat{A}, \cat{E})$ is an exact category with $\cat{A}$ small, there is an embedding (i.e. a faithful functor that is injective on the objects) $\cat{A} \xhookrightarrow{} \cat{B}$ into an abelian category such that $\cat{E}$ is the class of all short exact sequences in $\cat{B}$.
\end{rmk}

\subsection{Examples}
The previous statement is quite useful for finding exact categories that are not abelian categories. Here are a few fundamental general examples.

\begin{expls}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Let $R$ be a non-noetherian unital ring. Consider the category of finitely generated (left or right) $R$-modules $\text{mod}R$ (seen as a subcategory of $\text{Mod}R$). It is well known that this category is closed under extensions. However, it is not abelian. Indeed, consider a non-finitely generated ideal $I \subseteq R$ (which exists since $R$ is non-noetherian). Then the kernel of the quotient map is $R \xrath{} R/I$ is the ideal $I$. In that case, both $R$ and $R/I$ are finitely generated $R$-modules, but $I$ is by definition not finitely generated. Thus $\text{mod}R$ does not contain the kernel of all its maps, and cannot be abelian;
    \item Let $R$ be any ring. Consider the full subcategory of $\text{mod}R$ consisting of all projectives $\cat{P}(R)$. Let $P, P' \in \cat{P}(R)$ be two projective $R$-modules. It is well known from homological algebra that $\text{Ext}_R^1(P', P) = 0$. But then, considering $\text{Ext}_R^1$ as the Yoneda extension functor, this implies that any short exact sequence $0 \xra{} P \xra{} E \xra{} P' \xra{} 0$ splits, i.e. $E = P \oplus P'$. Since the direct sum of two projectives is projective, this shows that $\cat{P}(R)$ is closed under extensions. However, it is not necessarily abelian. For example, in the category $\text{mod}\mathbb{Z}$, the cokernel of the map $\mathbb{Z} \xra{\cdot 2} \mathbb{Z}$ is $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, which is not projective, so $\cat{P}(\mathbb{Z})$ is not abelian.
\end{itemize}
\end{expls}

\subsection{Reducing the axioms}
As was shown by B. Keller in \cite[Appendix~A]{Keller1990}, some of the axioms required in the classical definition of exact categories are redundant.

\begin{prop}
    Let $\cat{A}$ be an additive category and $\cat{E}$ be a class of kernel-cokernel pairs in $\cat{A}$ that is closed under isomorphisms. Assume that $\cat{E}$ satisfies \emph{[E1$\op$]}, \emph{[E2]} as well as

    \begin{itemize}[align = right]
        \item[\emph{[E0']}] $1_0$ is a deflation;
        \item[\emph{[E2$\op$']}] For any $f : C \rightarrow Z$ and deflation $d : Y \xrath{} Z$, there is a commutative square
